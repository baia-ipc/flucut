== unreleased ==

- the universal primers of the influenza A multisegment RT-PCR
  (Uni12/Uni13), built in: the conserved termini of the segments and the
  tailed primers of the protocols, each with the source of its sequence;
  a primer is stored as the whole oligonucleotide with the offset of its
  conserved terminus, as the tail and the terminus overlap in the
  published primers
- search of a primer in a read: alignment with errors, at the ends of
  the read also partial (a read can begin inside a primer), with the
  degenerate bases of the primers (e.g. the two forms of the Uni12
  primer) and of the reads; the bounds of the conserved terminus in the
  read are computed from the alignment path
- primer schemes: the primers expected at each end of an amplicon,
  built-in "mbt" (the tailed primers, the default) and "uni" (the
  conserved termini alone)
- internal priming: a primer is accepted as the primer of an end only
  within a window of that end of the read (default 100 bases), as a
  match deeper inside the read is not the primer which started the
  amplicon and trimming there would remove template; the matches
  between the two end primers are reported as internal matches (see
  docs/internal-priming.md)
- primer schemes: "uni12-mbt13" (the untailed degenerate Uni12 primer
  and the tailed Uni13 primer) is the default
- analysis of a read and trimming: both ends and both orientations, the
  reads are not reverse-complemented; the policy "primer" (the
  default) removes all the primer-derived bases, which also removes the
  internal priming artefact without references; "terminus" keeps the
  bases of the conserved termini
- reference sequences: FASTA reader; scripts/fetch_references.py
  downloads the segment references from NCBI with their provenance
- scripts/fetch_references.py: segments 7 (MP) and 8 (NS) are searched
  by the names of their genes (M1/M2, NS1/NEP/NS2), as NCBI annotates
  them, instead of MP[Gene] and NS[Gene], which matched no record: with
  --segment all, they were silently left out; test of the queries in
  make test (tests/test-fetch-references.py, --live for the download)
- scripts/fetch_references.py: a request answered by HTTP 429 (too many
  requests) is retried up to 5 times, with exponential backoff (1, 2, 4,
  8, 16 s) or the wait of the Retry-After header if longer (at most
  60 s), instead of stopping the download
- classification of a read against the references: the bases after the
  primer of each end are located in the segments (both strands, with
  errors and IUPAC codes); at a terminus the read is a normal amplicon,
  at an interior position it was internally primed; ambiguous reads are
  classified as terminal
- with the policy "terminus", the trimming is decided per read: the
  conserved termini of the normal amplicons are kept (only tails are
  removed), the whole primer of an internally primed end is removed;
  ends which cannot be located in the references are reported as
  unclassified; the references are then required (with the default
  policy they are optional and measure internal priming)
- command line program flucut: inputs FASTQ (optionally gzipped), BAM,
  or directories of such files (one output per directory); one gzipped
  FASTQ per input, ready for IRMA, with SAM-like tags in the titles
  (--no-tags to omit them); summary.tsv; options --trim, --references
  (required by --trim terminus), --max-errors, --window, --min-length,
  --threads (the output does not depend on it), --probe-len,
  --probe-errors, --terminal-window
- primer dimers are not written, also with --trim terminus (their
  conserved termini were kept)
- a read starting inside a degenerate primer is found without errors:
  the padding of the read now matches the IUPAC codes of the primer
- program tests on simulated reads from a real HA record (testdata/)
  and tests of the option validation, run by make test
- the classification against the references uses a k-mer index (the
  k-mer index module of libcbaia): only the windows around the
  candidate diagonals are aligned, and a read end without a candidate
  is searched exhaustively; same results as the exhaustive search on
  the benchmark reads, at a cost which hardly depends on the number of
  references; --exhaustive-search to check it
- length check of the complete amplicons: lengths.tsv (in bins of 10
  bases, per reference with --references), the complete amplicons
  shorter than 90% of their reference in summary.tsv (short_complete)
  and in the log, the peaks of the lengths in the log without
  references
- a primer is also found at a read end when its outer bases are
  missing (a read beginning inside the primer after a flank), and the
  outermost match near an end is the primer of that end (a
  primer-like sequence further inside is template): on the benchmark
  reads, fewer template bases removed and fewer primer and flank bases
  left
- the logo as colored text at the start of a run in a terminal (not
  with -q, -L, or when the messages are redirected)
- groups of input files (from Lumipore): a directory with barcode*
  subdirectories (e.g. the fastq_pass directory of a MinKNOW run) gives
  one output for each of them, the files beside them are not read (a
  warning); a directory without files of reads now gives a warning and
  an empty output, instead of an error; two groups with the same name
  are an error, as before
- counts for the reports (counter): besides the counts of summary.tsv
  and the lengths, the reads by primer found at each end, and, with
  references, the internally primed read ends by reference, end and
  position; per group and for all reads
- HTML report, always written: flucut_report.html in the output
  directory, self-contained (inline SVG plots, no external resources),
  light and dark themes, with the logo and colors of flucut (page,
  templates and plots of libcbaia, as in Lumipore): the run parameters;
  for all reads and for each group, the reads at each step, the primers
  found at each end, with references the read ends by origin and the
  internal priming sites with their reads, the complete amplicons (short
  ones per reference) and their length histograms
- JSON summary, always written: flucut_summary.json in the output
  directory, with the content of the HTML report, versioned (format 1,
  docs/json_summary.md), written to a temporary file and renamed; in
  the digest of the program tests
- the search of a primer at a read end with its outer bases missing is
  a single alignment (a dynamic programming of its own, the missing
  bases at a cost) instead of one alignment for each shorter form of
  the primer: half the time (0.7 s instead of 1.3 s for the 3200
  benchmark reads, one thread); of the best matches the outermost is
  taken: on the benchmark reads, fewer template, primer and flank
  bases wrongly removed or kept
- worker threads as in Lumipore (worker.h): the threads are started
  once for each file, each reads a batch of 1000 reads in turn under a
  lock, trims and compresses it with its own objects and writes it in
  the order of the input, waiting for its turn; the counts of each
  thread are summed at the end of the file. The output files are the
  same, byte for byte, for any -j (before, the last batch was split
  depending on -j); 32,000 reads with 8 references, 4 cores: 2.1-2.5 s
  with -j 4 instead of 3.0-3.2 s
- manual: chapter on the reports (with screenshots of the report of the
  simulated reads of the benchmark, docs/manual/figures/screenshot.js
  from Lumipore), the groups and the report files in the outputs
- messages: by default, one SUCCESS (green) line for each output, a
  WARNING (magenta) for many complete amplicons shorter than their
  reference, and a SUCCESS line for the reports written; with -v also
  the scheme and its primers, the input groups, the internally primed
  ends, the lengths without references and the tables written (INFO)
  and the step of writing the reports (PHASE)
- the segment of a reference (segment number and gene, from its FASTA
  description: "segment <n>" or a gene or protein name) in the warning
  of the short amplicons ("HA segment: 4 of 9 complete amplicons
  shorter than the closest reference (NC_004908.1, 1714 bases)..."), in
  the HTML report and in the JSON summary (references: segment, gene)
- adapter search (--adapters, default nb,rbk,lsk): at a read end
  without a primer, the adapters of Oxford Nanopore (native barcoding,
  rapid barcoding, ligation; sequences of Dorado, the barcode as Ns) are
  searched with the adaptor search of Lumipore (src/adaptor-search.c)
  and removed with everything outside them; the part between them is
  analyzed again, a primer next to an adapter removed as well; tags a5
  and a3, counts in the JSON summary (adapter_start, adapter_end) and in
  the HTML report; on the simulated barcoded reads, 698 adapter bases
  left instead of 28539, 1019 primer bases instead of 2221
- scripts/add-adapters.py and testdata/adapters.fastq: reads with
  adapters, for the program tests adapters and no_adapters
- -e auto (the default; from Lumipore, auto-settings.c): the errors in
  a primer decided from the error rate of the primer alignments in the
  first 1000 reads of the first input file: 0.2, or 0.3 from 4%
  (calibrated on reads simulated at 1-12% errors, benchmark/RESULTS.md);
  the value and the rate in the messages (-v) and in the reports
- benchmark/simulate.py --error-rate: the mean error rate of the reads
- live mode (--live, from the real-time mode of Lumipore, live.c): the
  files of the input directories trimmed as they are written (e.g. by
  MinKNOW), once not modified for --min-age (default 60 s) and complete;
  appended to the outputs; the tables and reports updated after each
  file (the HTML report reloads itself, the JSON summary "running":
  true); ends at the final summary of MinKNOW, after --timeout without
  new files, or with Ctrl-C; --scan-interval; --resume (the files
  trimmed and the counts of each sample saved in the output directory);
  tests/test-live.sh in make test
- the tables and reports written by a module of their own (results.c);
  the counts saved and loaded (counter_save(), counter_load())
- the output compressed with libdeflate (1.26, included in ext/libdeflate,
  also with SYSTEM_LIBS=1) instead of zlib, one gzip member for each
  batch as before (the decompressed output is unchanged); option
  --compress-level (1 to 12, default 6; compress_level in the settings
  of the JSON summary); 32,000 reads, 4 cores: 3.9 s instead of 6.6 s
  with -j 1, 1.6 s instead of 2.4 s with -j 4, an output 4% smaller
  (benchmark/RESULTS.md)
- -p, --platform nanopore|illumina: with illumina (reads of fragments of
  the amplicons, the primer at the read start), the window is 10 bases
  and the Nanopore adapters are not searched; in the JSON summary and
  the HTML report
- benchmark/run-real-illumina.sh: a public Illumina run (SRR34913296,
  H5N1 cattle) whose GenBank HA consensus has the I348K artefact: IRMA
  gives AAA on the untrimmed reads, ATA after flucut -p illumina, the
  only difference in the 8 segments
- -p illumina with --references: the read ends located by their k-mers
  only (reference_set_set_seeds_only()), without the exhaustive search
  of those the k-mers do not locate: 3 times faster on a run of milk
  samples (reads of the host), with the same output
